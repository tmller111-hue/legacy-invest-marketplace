04 · Our rehab standard

The same checklist on every house

Every property we acquire, renovate and list is updated to one written standard unless otherwise noted. It sets what gets repaired, what gets replaced, and the finishes, so a house in Memphis and a house in St. Louis come out the same.

Roof & exterior

Repair, or full tear-off with new decking as needed, underlayment, architectural shingles, flashing and drip edge. Siding repaired or new vinyl with white-wrapped trim. Gutters secured with extensions, trees trimmed to 10 feet off the roof, beds cleaned and mulched.

HVAC

Any furnace or condenser 10 years or older, or not working, is replaced. Condensers get a lockable cage, and every house gets a new digital thermostat.

Plumbing & water heater

Any water heater 10 years or older, or not working, is replaced, with a drip pan. The main stack and kitchen lines are jetted by a sewer company at final cleaning.

Electrical

Federal Pacific, Zinsco and Sylvania panels are replaced. GFCI in kitchens and baths, every switch and outlet working, and ceiling fans in the living room and bedrooms.

Safety

Interconnected smoke detectors in every bedroom and on every level, plus a carbon monoxide detector on each level.

Kitchens

New sink and faucet, cabinets painted or replaced in dark brown or black, and a stove, refrigerator and vent hood, plus a dishwasher where the layout allows.

Bathrooms

New toilet, vanity and faucets. Tubs are reglazed or replaced, with a new prefab or tile surround (we don't reglaze tile surrounds). A vent fan goes in wherever there's no window.

Floors, doors & windows

Hardwoods refinished or LVP installed; LVP in kitchens, baths and laundry; all carpet removed. Bronze hardware, weather stripping, and every window opens, closes and locks.

Basements

Cleaned out, with floors and stairs painted and glass block windows. Where we add a bedroom, it gets an egress window and is at least 120 square feet.

Licensed contractors do the electrical, plumbing and HVAC work, and permits are turned in to our project manager. Each house is signed off on a quality control checklist and deep cleaned so it's ready to show to a tenant.

05 · Preliminary scope of work

A documented plan before the work begins

When you reserve a home that is being renovated, you receive a preliminary scope of work. It describes in plain language exactly what we intend to do to the house before it's turned over to you, trade by trade and room by room. It's issued early, before work starts, so you're buying a documented plan rather than a promise.

It's preliminary for a reason. The scope is written from our initial condition assessment and can change once demolition exposes hidden conditions and licensed trades finish their evaluations. Any material change is sent to you as a revised scope before that work is performed.

What we touch on the house

Ten systems are addressed on every full renovation.

  1. Roof system. Tear-off to decking, new architectural shingles, underlayment, drip edge and boots.
  2. Attic & insulation. Insulation replaced or upgraded, ventilation verified, attic access sealed.
  3. Gutters, fascia & soffit. Rotten wood replaced; seamless gutters with downspouts discharged away from the foundation.
  4. Siding & exterior paint. Damaged siding replaced, surfaces prepped and painted.
  5. Windows & exterior doors. Broken glass and failed sashes replaced; new locksets, weatherstripping and thresholds.
  6. HVAC. System evaluated or replaced, ductwork sealed, new thermostat, condenser pad and cage.
  7. Electrical. Panel and service inspected, deficiencies corrected, devices and fixtures replaced.
  8. Plumbing & water heater. Lines pressure tested, leaks corrected, water heater serviced or replaced.
  9. Interior finishes. Drywall repair, full interior paint, new flooring, trim, doors and hardware.
  10. Foundation & grading. Structural items addressed, crawlspace cleared with vapor barrier, grading pulled away from the house.

How the work gets done

Our in-house construction division follows the same eight stages on every house. Each stage produces a record (a photo set, a permit, an inspection result or a signed checklist), and those records back the warranties.

  1. Condition assessment

    The property is walked and photographed in full. Every deficiency is documented with a dated photo before a dollar is spent.

  2. Scope written and priced

    The preliminary scope is produced line by line by trade, priced by our own construction division rather than a third-party bid.

  3. Permits pulled

    Permits are pulled in every jurisdiction that requires them. No permit-required work happens without one.

  4. Before photos

    A complete before set is filed so the finished condition can be checked against the starting condition.

  5. Rehab executed

    Trades are sequenced: demo and trashout, roof and exterior dry-in, mechanical rough-in, drywall, paint, flooring, cabinets and fixtures, punch list.

  6. Trade inspections

    Plumbing, electrical, HVAC and building inspections are scheduled and passed where required.

  7. Quality control walkthrough

    An internal QC checklist is walked and signed off by construction management before the job is called complete.

  8. Documentation and handoff

    After photos are filed, warranties are activated and the property is turned over rent-ready to property management.

07 · The buying process

From first look to first rent check

Here is how a purchase moves, based on our standard purchase contract. The contract you sign controls, so read it in full.

  1. Browse and run the numbers

    Every listing on our marketplace has an editable pro forma. Change the down payment, rate, vacancy and reserves to match how you invest.

  2. Reserve the property

    Submit a reservation request from the listing. A reservation is not final until you receive written confirmation from us.

  3. Sign the purchase contract within 2 business days

    We send the contract through PandaDoc for e-signature. If it isn't signed within 2 business days of being sent, it becomes voidable. You can assign the contract to your LLC or corporation.

  4. Deposit earnest money within 72 hours

    Earnest money goes to the title company named in the contract. Until it's received, the contract isn't binding and the home stays on the market.

  5. Choose how you're paying

    Cash purchases have no financing contingency. Financed purchases are contingent on your loan; if you're denied after acting in good faith, a lender denial letter is required for your earnest money to be returned. Private-money buyers send a pre-qualification letter right away and complete loan documents within 10 days.

  6. Review the scope of work

    For homes being renovated, you receive a preliminary scope of work after contract signing. Review it within 72 hours and send us any questions.

  7. Complete due diligence in 5 business days

    You have 5 business days for inspections. On an already-renovated home, due diligence starts when you sign. On a home being renovated, it starts when we email you that the renovation is complete. We can order a licensed third-party home inspection ($450) and pest inspection ($200) for you, or you can hire your own inspector.

  8. Appraisal and loan approval

    You may use any lender. If your lender's appraisal comes in below the price and a rebuttal doesn't fix it, you agree to switch to one of our recommended lenders, and we pay for the new appraisal.

  9. Bind insurance

    A landlord policy must be in force at closing, with your lender listed as mortgagee. You can request a quote from our Goosehead agency.

  10. Close within 60 days of loan approval

    We close with the title company by warranty deed. Taxes, rents and security deposits are prorated, each side pays its normal closing costs, and on a rented home the leasing fee is reimbursed to the seller. You can do a final walkthrough before closing.

  11. Hand off to management

    Sign the management agreement in PandaDoc and you'll receive your Propertyware owner portal login. The property team takes it from there.

Know these contract terms. Legacy Invest Realty represents the seller, not the buyer. Earnest money is non-refundable after the inspection period, except for a financing denial. If closing isn't completed within 60 days of loan approval, earnest money is forfeited unless we extend in writing. There are no credits, buydowns or earnest money refunds if the process takes longer than expected.

Terms you'll see on every listing

Gross rent
The monthly rent on the lease, or market rent for a vacant home.
Vacancy
An allowance for months the home sits empty between tenants or rent that isn't collected. Often modeled at 5–8% of rent.
Effective income
Gross rent minus vacancy. This is what you realistically collect.
Operating expenses
Taxes, insurance, management and a repairs reserve. The mortgage is not an operating expense.
NOI
Net operating income: effective income minus operating expenses. It measures the property itself, independent of how you finance it.
Cap rate
Annual NOI divided by purchase price. Useful for comparing properties on equal terms, whether you pay cash or finance.
Debt service
Your monthly loan payment of principal and interest.
Cash flow
NOI minus debt service. The money left for you each month.
Cash-on-cash return
Annual cash flow divided by the cash you put in (down payment plus closing costs). It shows what your invested dollars are earning.
Capital expenditures
Big-ticket replacements such as a roof, HVAC or water heater. Our renovations reset many of these, but a long-term owner should still set money aside.
09 · Financing

Ways to pay for a rental

Investment property loans work differently from buying a home to live in. Down payments are larger, rates are usually a little higher, and lenders look closely at the property's rent. Here are the common routes our buyers take.

10 · 1031 exchanges

Trading up without paying tax today

A 1031 exchange lets you sell an investment property and defer capital gains tax by reinvesting the proceeds into another investment property. Turnkey rentals are a common replacement property because they're ready to produce income and can close on a predictable schedule.

Day 0 · you sellDay 45Day 180
Identify replacement properties in writingClose on the replacement property
  • Use a qualified intermediary (QI). Sale proceeds must go to the QI, not to you. Set up the QI before your sale closes.
  • 45 days to identify. Replacement properties must be identified in writing within 45 days of selling the old property.
  • 180 days to close. The purchase must close within 180 days of the sale (or by your tax filing deadline, if that comes first).
  • Match or exceed to defer fully. To defer all the tax, buy property of equal or greater value and reinvest all the net proceeds. Cash you keep, or debt you don't replace, is "boot" and is taxable.
  • Spread it across several homes. You can exchange one larger property into several turnkey rentals, which diversifies across tenants and neighborhoods.
Plan ahead with our team. Tell us your sale date and your 45-day deadline. We'll help you identify properties that can close inside your 180-day window. Always confirm the details of your exchange with your QI and CPA.
11 · Insurance

A rental needs a landlord policy

A standard homeowners policy is written for a home you live in. A rental needs a landlord policy, often called a dwelling fire policy (DP-3 is the most complete form). It covers the structure, your liability as an owner, and lost rent while the home is repaired after a covered loss.

What to look for

Adequate dwelling coverage for the home, loss of rents, premises liability of at least $1,000,000, and a deductible you can comfortably cover from reserves.

Your tenant's side

Your policy doesn't cover the tenant's belongings. Our lease encourages tenants to carry renters insurance for their own property.

Growing a portfolio

As you add homes, an umbrella policy adds liability protection across all of them for a relatively low cost.

Through our Goosehead Insurance agency we shop landlord coverage across multiple carriers, and we already know the properties and their updates, so we can help you choose adequate coverage. Our pro forma estimates insurance at 1% of the purchase price. Get a real quote before closing to replace that estimate.

14 · Our markets

Memphis and St. Louis

Both cities offer purchase prices well below the national median alongside steady rental demand, which is why rents can support a positive cash flow in markets like these. Each has its own rules and quirks.

Memphis, Tennessee

Tennessee has no state income tax on wages. Homes inside Memphis city limits pay both City of Memphis and Shelby County property taxes, so always check the tax line on the pro forma. Rental performance varies street by street, so we price and rent each home based on its immediate area.

St. Louis, Missouri

St. Louis City and St. Louis County are separate jurisdictions, and many municipalities in the region have their own occupancy permit or inspection requirements when a new tenant moves in. Our management team handles those requirements as part of leasing.

Local ordinances change. We keep up with the requirements that apply to our properties, and you should confirm anything that affects your decision.

16 · After closing

Your first year as an owner

  • Keep a cash reserve. Hold a few months of expenses per property for repairs, turnover and vacancy so a surprise never becomes a problem.
  • Read your monthly statements. Log in to your Propertyware owner portal and compare actual rent and expenses to the pro forma. Questions are easier to resolve early.
  • Watch your tax bill and insurance renewal. Your lender may escrow them, but reassessments and premium changes still affect your cash flow.
  • Take depreciation. Residential rental buildings are generally depreciated over 27.5 years, which can offset much of your rental income on paper. Your CPA will set this up.
  • Keep your records. Save your closing statement, scope of work, inspection and insurance policy together. You'll need them at tax time and when you sell or exchange.
  • Plan the next one. Many of our investors buy again once their first property is stable. We'll help you look at refinancing, equity and your next purchase.

18 · FAQ

Common questions

Why is a home under renovation listed at its finished price?

You're reserving the house now and buying it once the renovation is complete. The price on the website is the price of the finished home, so it already includes the renovation. After contract signing you'll receive a preliminary scope of work showing what's being done, and your due diligence starts when we let you know the work is finished.

When do I inspect a home that's still being renovated?

Due diligence happens once renovations are completed, so your third-party home inspection and your lender's appraisal look at the finished home you're actually buying.

Can I buy if I live in another state?

Yes. Most of the process runs remotely: e-signed contracts, video walkthroughs, remote or mobile-notary closings and professional management after you close.

Is my reservation binding?

A reservation request holds your place in line while we confirm details. It isn't final until you receive written confirmation, and the purchase contract sets the actual terms.

Can I see examples of your work?

Yes. Our work has before and after photos and full scopes of work for recent projects, and Documents has our rehab standard, management agreements and sample lease.

What happens after I get the scope of work?

Read it and send us any questions. If we don't hear back within 72 hours of sending it, we consider you satisfied with the information and photos.

Can I use my own lender, inspector or insurance agent?

Yes. You can choose your own lender, inspector and insurance agent. If your lender's appraisal comes in low and a rebuttal doesn't resolve it, the contract requires switching to one of our recommended lenders, and we pay for the new appraisal. For the smoothest transaction, we recommend using our vetted team of lenders, inspectors and insurance agents, who already know our properties and our process.

Does Legacy Invest represent me as the buyer?

No. Legacy Invest Realty represents the seller. The owners of Legacy Invest also own the management company and the Goosehead insurance agency that may serve you, and each may be paid separately. You'll sign a disclosure explaining these relationships.

Is the renovation warrantied?

Yes. Legacy Invest homes come with a 1-year mechanical warranty on the roof, HVAC and hot water tank, and a 90-day maintenance warranty on the completed scope of work, both starting at closing. See the preliminary scope of work section for what is and isn't covered.

When is my earnest money refundable?

During the 5-business-day inspection period, or if you're financing and your loan is denied (with a denial letter). After that it isn't refundable, and it's forfeited if closing doesn't happen within 60 days of loan approval.
